Iram Lamiya Hoque is a Ph.D. student in Civil and Environmental Engineering at the University of Washington, advised by [Prof. Brett Maurer](https://www.ce.washington.edu/facultyfinder/brett-maurer). Lamiya is building an open-source AI model for global coseismic landslide susceptibility, trained on 1–2 million observations with 90 geospatial predictors, that turns USGS ShakeMaps into landslide probability maps for rapid hazard response.

The research sits at the intersection of Earth science, data science, and civil engineering: from triaxial and shake-table testing of soils to machine-learning models that run at global scale on high-performance computing.

Before UW, Lamiya taught from 2018 to 2022, as a Lecturer at Ahsanullah University of Science and Technology and then as a Lecturer and Assistant Professor of Teaching at the Japan Institute of Disaster Prevention and Urban Safety, BUET. Lamiya holds an M.Sc. (2019) and a B.Sc. (2017) in Civil Engineering from Bangladesh University of Engineering and Technology (BUET).
